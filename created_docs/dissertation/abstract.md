# Abstract

Small and medium enterprises employ close to half of Sri Lanka's workforce but administer human resources manually, and the AI-driven platforms that could give them early warning of employee resignation are priced for enterprise budgets. This dissertation designs, builds and evaluates a cost-effective predictive HR analytics framework for firms of twenty to fifty employees, integrating a serverless attrition-prediction service into a production HR platform under an operational ceiling of LKR 10,000 per month.

Following Design Science Research, two models were trained and compared. A **transfer model** was fitted to 2,820 training records — 2,320 real international and 500 calibrated synthetic — and validated against held-out Sri Lankan data using only the four features the sources share. A **local model** was fitted within 230 Sri Lankan records on eight psychometric constructs. Both are served from an IAM-locked, scale-to-zero inference endpoint returning per-prediction SHAP explanations, and the constructs are captured inside the product through a weekly micro-survey, so that the stronger model has a route to its own inputs.

The central result is negative, and is reported as the principal finding. Attrition patterns do not meaningfully transfer across national contexts: the cross-context model reaches ROC-AUC 0.641 against the local model's 0.937, and decomposition shows that age and gender together score 0.457 — below chance — while the transfer model's residual skill comes almost entirely from satisfaction items that are, on the validation side, self-reported in the same instrument as the outcome. Synthetic augmentation contributes +0.032 ROC-AUC, within seed noise, and the sample-weighting scheme central to the data strategy proves immaterial at −0.007. Operational cost was measured rather than estimated at **LKR 4,050 per month**: inference is effectively free at 150 billable instance-seconds across four months, while the one component that cannot scale to zero, the database, accounts for roughly 70% of spend. Usability was not measured; the planned user study was replaced by expert inspection and the objective is reported as unmeasured rather than met.

Two limitations qualify every predictive claim: the Sri Lankan outcome is turnover *intention* rather than observed departure, and predictors and outcome derive from a single self-report instrument. The work contributes an empirical demonstration that international data and calibrated synthetic augmentation cannot substitute for local data collection in this domain, a measured serverless cost baseline for SME-scale predictive analytics, and a deployed mechanism for capturing a psychometric model's inputs in production.

**Keywords:** employee attrition prediction, HR analytics, explainable AI, SHAP, serverless computing, cost-effective machine learning, transfer learning, turnover intention, small and medium enterprises, Sri Lanka
